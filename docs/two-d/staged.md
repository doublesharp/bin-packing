# Native staged guillotine packing

`two_d::staged::solve_2d_staged` constructs a physical cut tree while packing.
Each operation splits its complete rectangular material region. Placements are
exact part leaves of that tree. The solver uses Rust throughout; PackingSolver
is a reference, with no C++ runtime or FFI dependency.

The API accepts `StagedProblem2D`, containing the existing
`ConstrainedProblem2D`, machine rules, and optional source-demand group IDs.
`StagedSolution2D` includes ordinary layouts, stock and demand indices, per-source
unplaced quantities, indexed cut trees and their canonical production sequence.
Indices are authoritative even when several source records have identical names.

## Physical model

Each tree starts at node zero with the usable rectangle remaining after the four
specified trim borders have already been removed. Trim preparation is outside the
tree and the stage/count limits. Coordinates remain relative to the original
sheet. `waste_area` includes trims, defects, unused material and blade loss.

For a cut, `offset` measures from the material region's low edge to the near
blade edge. A vertical cut consumes `kerf × region.height`; a horizontal cut
consumes `kerf × region.width`. Children are the exact nonempty rectangles on
either side, ordered low coordinate first. Positive kerf may leave just one
material child when the blade reaches an edge. Zero kerf requires a strictly
internal cut with two children. Every physical cut, including a finishing cut,
is retained; every material node is reachable exactly once. A part fills its
leaf completely. Edge kerf relief is unsupported and rejected explicitly.

Parts avoid both rectangular defects and a conservative kerf clearance, matching
the constrained rectangular API. Independently, `cut_through_defects: false`
prohibits a blade band or zero-thickness line from crossing a defect's interior.
Touching its boundary is allowed. Enabling the flag permits those cuts while
still excluding defective material from parts. Defects may overlap trims or
other defects.

Usable-drop metrics are computed separately for each terminal waste piece after
subtracting its defects. Waste pieces are never merged across a physical cut,
including a cut with zero kerf. Removed trim strips are not available drops.
Squared-area totals saturate at the existing `u128` reporting boundary.

## Stages and machine limits

A stage is one run of cuts along the same axis on a root-to-leaf path. Changing
axis adds a stage; parallel successive cuts retain the stage number. The first
configured axis defines nominal stage one. A first physical cut along the other
axis uses nominal stage two, representing an empty first stage. `Any` lets each
sheet choose a concrete nominal first axis. `max_stages: null` removes the stage
limit; finite limits must be between one and sixteen.

The four cut policies use the terminal-block semantics checked in the pinned
[PackingSolver solution validator](https://github.com/fontanf/packingsolver/blob/376b8d1cc4aed5b171bfe1219c037c661faaa7cf/src/rectangleguillotine/solution.cpp)
and [native reference branching scheme](https://github.com/fontanf/packingsolver/blob/376b8d1cc4aed5b171bfe1219c037c661faaa7cf/src/rectangleguillotine/tree_search.cpp).

| Policy | Stage accounting |
| --- | --- |
| `exact` | Every physical axis level counts. |
| `non_exact` | The deepest level is discounted once only if **every** block at that level contains one part and at most one waste piece. |
| `roadef2018` | The same discount also permits a terminal block of two parts and at most one waste piece. |
| `homogeneous` | Exact accounting, with a single source demand type in each final nominal-stage strip. With an unlimited stage count, the deepest actual stage is the final stage. |

The discount never removes an operation from the physical tree or its kerf and
cut-count accounting. The Roadef implementation uses the one-or-two-part model
generated by the reference; it does not infer eligibility merely from matching
part heights. A pattern cannot obtain an extra free level by putting a regular
multi-part strip beside a qualifying finishing block.

`minimum_distance_1_cuts` and `maximum_distance_1_cuts` bound the material width
of each productive stage-one strip along its cutting axis. The corresponding
stage-two fields bound productive stage-two strips along that axis. Bounds
include finished part strips; terminal waste is governed separately by
`minimum_waste_length`, which applies to both sides of every nonempty waste
piece. An empty nominal first stage has one full-width productive strip. These
explicit physical-strip bounds also work with a two-stage limit; they are a
generalization of the reference's restricted parameter combinations.

`maximum_number_1_cuts` counts physical stage-one cuts per sheet.
`maximum_number_2_cuts` counts physical stage-two cuts per first-level subplate.
Residual separation and finishing operations count when physically performed at
those stages. These limits constrain cuts rather than the number of parts.

Group IDs align with source demands, or the group vector may be empty. Within a
group, **all copies of each earlier source demand must be produced before any
copy of a later source demand**. Different groups may interleave. This follows
the reference's source-type predecessor rule. The declared production order is
the low-before-high depth-first traversal of part leaves, in sheet order; the
validator reconstructs it. This API does not model simultaneous bundle sawing.

## Construction, search and validation

The baseline tries demand orientations, both orders of perpendicular isolation
cuts and all four corners of each available waste leaf. Opening a sheet follows
stock inventory. Each accepted movement changes the cut tree itself. The beam
also explores mixed demand orders and skipping a demand when supply or machine
rules prevent fulfillment. Both count and cost objectives affect construction
and incumbent selection; fulfillment has priority over either material objective.

`beam_width` is one through 32. `max_states` is zero through 100,000 and bounds
evaluated beam successors, including rejected candidates and skip alternatives.
Greedy construction is outside that successor budget. A zero budget returns a
validated greedy incumbent; tiny budgets preserve that incumbent. A bounded
128-move shortlist and beam pruning set `search_truncated` when alternatives are
omitted. No value of that flag proves global optimality. The solver returns a
valid partial result with exact unplaced source quantities when necessary.

The supported problem size is 128 total parts, 64 demand types, 16 stock types
and 128 defects. The search is a deterministic heuristic over corner-isolation
trees. It does not enumerate every legal guillotine partition, introduce
arbitrary sacrificial cuts to reach an interior placement, or prove infeasibility
when a demand remains unplaced. It does not claim universal performance or
quality superiority over PackingSolver.

`validate_2d_staged` independently reconstructs cut partitions and blade bands,
material conservation, reachability, direction/stage limits, productive-strip
distances, physical cut counts, finishing and homogeneous blocks, defects,
prepared trims, source quantities, rotation, stock supply, group sequence,
placements and all material/cost/drop totals. It trusts no cut-generation state.
Malformed controls return `InvalidConstraint`; corrupt trees or accounting
return `InvalidSolution`.

Fixtures cover a full-area pattern needing three stages instead of two, a
binding first axis, differing cut policies, kerf and defect cut-through,
per-side trims, distances and cut counts, ordered groups, duplicate labels,
cost/count selection, zero and tiny budgets, serialization and corrupted trees.

Effective option defaults are recorded in the [generated reference](../reference.md).
